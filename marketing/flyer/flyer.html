Your Fleet Maintenance Manager

Know what you're spending on your fleet's maintenance, and why.

Fleet OTG replaces spreadsheets, phone calls and chat approvals with one job card for every repair. Here is what goes wrong, and how it is fixed.

1

Every approval on record

ProblemRepairs get approved by chat or phone call, and nobody can say later who said yes.

FixJob cards, quotations and invoices are approved with the person's name and the time. Only the administrator may decide on their own request, and it is recorded.

2

One card per repair, even offline

ProblemHistory is scattered across spreadsheets, receipts and chats, and written up days later.

FixOne job card per repair, with every step logged. Fill the Excel form in at the workshop with no internet and upload it later.

3

Workshops quote from a phone

ProblemQuotes arrive as phone calls and photos, then get lost or disputed.

FixThe workshop gets a one-time link on its own phone number and sends its estimate, recorded against the job card number.

4

Invoices checked before payment

ProblemA workshop's invoice comes in higher than the price you agreed.

FixEvery invoice is checked against the approved quotation before payment, and whoever approved the card cannot check its invoice.

5

Cost lands on the right vehicle

ProblemNobody knows what each vehicle really costs, or who had it at the time.

FixSpend is counted per vehicle and per period against its budget, and follows who held the vehicle.

6

Repeat repairs are flagged

ProblemThe same fault is paid for again and again.

FixA fault coming back on the same vehicle is shown, so you can see what keeps failing and why.

7

Drivers see their own vehicle

ProblemDrivers ask the office what their vehicle cost, and access would show them the whole fleet.

FixA driver sees only the job cards of the vehicle they hold and what it has cost, and nothing else.

8

A real person behind every action

ProblemShared logins mean no action can be traced to anyone.

FixEvery login is tied to an employee code and a role, so each action traces back to a real person.
