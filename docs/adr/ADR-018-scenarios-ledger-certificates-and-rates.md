# ADR-018: Tax scenarios, an internal ledger, withholding certificates and dated exchange rates

Status: accepted, 2026-10-24. Extends ADR-017. Owner choices: customers outside Pakistan sign up tax free; the application owner configures tax scenarios; the application issues withholding certificates itself; a double-entry ledger for the subscription business; dollar-to-rupee conversion at the interbank rate on the payment date.

## Not tax or legal advice
Nothing here has been checked by a tax adviser or a lawyer. See "To confirm" below.

## Decisions
1. **Country.** A customer states its country at sign-up (a list of countries, required on the form). It is kept in the customer's tax record (`org_tax.country`). It is self-declared, so the operator sees it on the organization page and on every payment awaiting confirmation, and can correct it. A customer with no country is treated as before: no tax lines.
2. **Scenarios** (`tax_scenarios`, edited on the operator's Tax page). A scenario says what a customer gets: no tax, the rate of the authority the customer chooses (Pakistan), or one fixed rate; whether the invoice is in dollars or in rupees at a dated rate; whether withholding applies; the invoice title and a note. The first active scenario by priority whose country list contains the customer's country applies; one with no list matches everyone. Two ship as a starting point: customers in Pakistan, and everyone else with no tax. The owner can change them and add more (for example a country with VAT). A taxed scenario that is not fully set up (no registration number, no authority, no rate) charges nothing rather than guessing.
3. **Dated exchange rates** (`fx_rates`, the operator's Rates page). A rupee invoice converts at the rate for the day the customer paid (the transfer date on the claim). A day with no rate uses the latest earlier day within a week; beyond that it falls back to the manual rate in Settings, and the invoice records where its rate came from. Which side (buying, selling, average) is a setting. The operator can always enter a rate by hand; that is the dependable route.
4. **forex.pk.** The owner asked for the rate from forex.pk. Its terms of use forbid commercial use of the site without its prior written consent, and it calls its rates indicative. The automatic fetch (a daily scheduled job and a "fetch now" button) therefore stays **off** until the owner records, in Settings, who gave consent and when. With no consent on record nothing is requested. Fetched rates are stored under the date the page states, never over a rate entered by hand, and a jump of more than 10% from the previous stored rate is refused as a likely misreading. Past dates cannot be fetched (the site's archive did not respond to a scripted request); the history builds up from the first fetch, and earlier dates are entered by hand. An official source such as the State Bank of Pakistan may suit better; the fetcher is one small module.
5. **Ledger** (`acct_accounts`, `acct_journals`, `acct_lines`; the operator's Accounting page). Every invoice posts one balanced journal in the same transaction that issues it. Entries are never edited or deleted (the database refuses); a mistake is corrected by a reversing entry. Each journal is in one currency (a dollar invoice and a rupee invoice do not mix; the trial balance is per currency and each balances on its own). The owner sets the chart of accounts; posting goes to the accounts that carry a role (bank, revenue, sales tax payable, income tax withheld, sales tax withheld, short and over), so accounts can be renamed or renumbered. Tax withheld by a customer is an asset (recoverable), not cash. A payment that is short or over posts the difference to its own account. Manual adjustments and a backfill for older invoices are available. A CSV of the journal and of the tax summary is available for the accountant.
6. **Withholding certificates issued by the application** (`wht_certificates`). For an invoice where the customer withheld tax, the customer (from its invoice page) or the operator (for a customer) prepares a numbered certificate (WHC-YYYY-NNNNNN) from the figures on the invoice, with the customer's and the supplier's details as they were, the section the owner has configured, and the challan or payment slip the tax was deposited with (required when the customer issues it). It is fixed once issued; a mistake is voided and replaced. Issuing it records the certificate as held on the invoice, so the operator's "certificates owed" list clears.
7. **What the certificate is not.** In Pakistan the statutory statement of tax withheld is issued and filed by the withholding agent through the tax authority's system. This certificate is a document made by Fleet VMMS from the invoice and the deposit details the issuer enters, and it says so on its face. Whether the supplier or the tax authority will accept it in place of the withholding agent's own certificate is a question for the accountant.

## To confirm with a tax adviser
All the items in ADR-017, and:
- Whether customers outside Pakistan are indeed outside the scope of Pakistani sales tax for this service, and whether any statement or evidence must be kept (for example of the customer's location).
- Whether a rate for the payment date from the source chosen, on the side chosen, is what the tax authority expects, or the rate on the invoice date.
- Whether a document generated like the certificate here is acceptable to the supplier's tax filing, or whether the customer must always give its own.
- How the ledger's accounts map to the supplier's actual books (an individual's records may differ from a company's).

## Consequences
- The country is trusted but visible: a Pakistani company that declares another country would be invoiced without tax until the operator corrects it. The payment screen names the country and scenario so this is seen before confirming.
- Several invoices already issued before this change carry no scenario and are listed as such in the tax summary.
- Credit notes still do not exist. A wrong invoice cannot be edited; the ledger can be corrected by reversal, but the invoice itself cannot.
- The certificate numbering and the ledger numbering are sequences: a rolled-back transaction can leave a gap. The numbers identify, they do not prove completeness.
